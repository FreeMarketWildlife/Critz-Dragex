# Cosmology, magic, and society

**Status:** Recovered, version-specific written lore. Current explicit author decisions override it. Source IDs link through [sources.md](sources.md). These sections are not one silently reconciled creation account.

## 1. The latest recovered whole-history outline — C01, April 2026

The **Creator** makes the star-filled heavens, then creates **Dragex and Moana** and gives them creative powers with the responsibility to live in love and harmony. Dragex makes the land and its creatures. Moana makes oceans, lakes, and aquatic creatures. **Birds are their joint creation.** Dragex resents the water covering his rock foundation, attacks Moana, and she dies of grief. Her single Tear remains at the site later called Death's Valley.

The source describes **death as the final thing they create together**. Dragex cries himself to sleep and covers himself in obsidian, producing a stratovolcano at **Mount Yotei**. The same document later uses **Mount Yokai** for the Fey battle. Do not silently resolve those names. This source establishes the commissioned creators but does not by itself establish every earlier claim about their being siblings, an eye being torn out, or their exact animal forms.

With Moana dead and Dragex asleep, the Creator appoints **Fey** to govern/steward the world. The Fey divide into factions and fight a catastrophic battle on the snowy summit of Mount Yokai. Their blood has two effects: **consumption changes animals**, including speech and upright behavior; **drying crystallizes it into arcane**, tangible and workable magic.

The **Spring of Life** is the snowmelt that carries Fey blood into the mountain's rivers. Animals drink and change. The source describes the period after the Fey are gone as initially better, with transformed animals treating the world more respectfully.

**Koric** is a salamander who drank in that Spring of Life. He lives in the lake where Death's Valley will be, takes Moana's sustaining Tear from its center, and hides it. He is punished with the **phoenix curse**: he cannot truly die until the Tear is returned. Its return prevents the destruction from spreading across all Hokkaido.

Later, prosperity turns to progress, ambition, and sin. Nations develop arcane weapons and destroy one another in an arcane equivalent of nuclear warfare. Post-apocalyptic fallout follows. The Creator eventually judges the living and dead, sinks the old Hokkaido, makes a new harmonious Hokkaido, and removes death. The last age is enduring peace and exploration of new islands. See the [age register](../../timeline/seven-ages.md) for the source's seven labels and its conflict with the current Age-4 novel opening.

## 2. Allohim and critter civilization — N05, May 2026

The latest recovered opening uses the name **Allohim** for the **Sustainer**. Cooperation that glorifies the Sustainer is contrasted with self-glorification. The narrator describes destiny as each critter being asked to use free will to participate in Allohim's plan. The April outline calls its supreme figure “the Creator”; the exact canonical relationship and final naming should be confirmed rather than treated as an author answer to a question not yet asked.

N05 distinguishes **critters** from **wild animals** through names, a tendency toward bipedal movement, complex structures, and civilizations. Its narrator says wild animals are not given names. This is a rule in that version, not a universal fact we can impose on older stories that name a wild panther cub or other animals.

Rootport's economy is described concretely: the Parsley household raises worms and maggots; Veratus collects waste, composts it into nutrient-rich soil, and carries it to fruit farms. Different workers support one another. The older farmer Roger resists imported shortcuts. Preserve this as village culture and characterization, not advice about real animal care or agriculture.

## 3. Earlier creator account — H02 and T03, 2021

The earlier account uses **Makani / Behemoth** for the ruler of the heavens and flying creatures and **Moana / Leviathan** for the oceans and swimming creatures. Makani raises Hokkaido so his creatures can rest. Moana becomes enclosed in its central lake; she cracks the land and creates channels to reconnect with the sea. Makani is angered by the rivers separating his land creatures, attacks her, and she dies of grief after shedding a tear.

**H02** says the lake dries when Moana dies and Makani raises a mountain into which he enters to die of remorse. **C01** instead has Dragex sleeping beneath obsidian and the lake sustained until Koric removes the Tear. These are material differences in both names and causal sequence. Do not simply replace every “Makani” with “Dragex,” or assume a sleeping being and a dead one are the same state.

## 4. Tear rules under development — T03, B02, H01, X05

T03 contains both proposed rules and the author's questions about whether they work. Recoverable ideas include the Tear sustaining nearby life without ordinary food/water/breath needs, dependency after roughly three days of exposure, and an effect affecting lake creatures over a much longer period. Contact with water calls Moana's aquatic children to vengeance. Those numerical/rule ideas are not all reconfirmed by C01.

The proposed ending in T03 explores whether placing water on the Tear could be an act of faith, and whether a Tear fragment in an arrow through Koric's heart could bring him back. The source itself questions consistency; do not publish those speculative solutions as events that definitely happen.

In **B02**, Psyte/Pyste carries a glowing turquoise, vein-cracked stone. It attracts the blue amulet on Koric's necklace. The full chapter explicitly calls the stone **Moana's Tear** and has Koric say he will restore life to Death's Valley. A sea-creature attack destroys Weston; Psyte hands the Tear to Koric, and Díkaios rescues Koric and Veratus. This acquisition differs from the later guild-bargain and Sapporo-heist plots.

In **H01**, set 144 years after the Tear's removal, Archaíos finds an apparently dead red-and-black salamander on Eden's Isle. He places a blue-gem necklace on him and lights a funeral fire; the salamander cries out and climbs from the flames. The necklace and fire are present, but the short fragment does not fully explain their respective causal roles. A note says “Moana's Tear” can name an **event as well as an object**.

In **X05**, an alternative curse says that if Koric dies without fighting his hardest, he goes into an eternal dark abyss. This is a fragment and potentially conflicts with C01's unqualified “cannot truly die until the Tear is returned.” It is not a settled extra condition.

## 5. Earlier civilization model — T14 and T11

T14's early chronology contains the **Seventh Shima War**, **Akuma Yōkai**, the **Battle of Oshidashi**, and fairy survivors returning to **Hachimori, the City of Eight Woods**. Blood from the snow spreads after a volcanic eruption. Animals form tribes, speak, and later develop altered anatomy.

A dwarven expedition arrives from the south thirteen years later. A young dwarf teaches the animals a shared language and distributes literature. Sapporo is founded in 555 AC. This is an older account of language/civilization, not a confirmed sequel to C01's Fey stewardship story.

T11 calls the common tongue **Hongo**, uses **chisana** for creatures roughly between ants and squirrels in size, and **yahse** for a creature outside that linguistic/civic society. It also sketches a dwarf called **Hok**, an old song, **Kaido**, a curse, and a separate fragment in which Koric spreads a common tongue over a millennium and establishes **Eastlake**.

Crucially, T11 also imagines Koric remembering earlier human rulers leaving Hokkaido. **That premise is superseded by Phil's explicit current statement: no humans in Hokkaido during the novels; arrival only much later from the west.** The user has not separately settled every older fairy/dwarf/gnome concept. Do not assume either that all survive or that the no-human rule automatically excludes all nonhuman magical beings.

## 6. Society and prejudice — T15, N02, R01, R03

T15 describes an old hierarchy: birds are revered as angelic and outside the ordinary functional classes; mammals are the ruling group; reptiles are former rulers; amphibians are even older rulers associated with banned magic and subjected to segregation. It describes insects/fish as farmed wildlife, snails similarly, bivalves as mysterious, and squid/octopuses as feared sea beings.

These statements describe **in-world beliefs, power structures, and prejudice**, not objective moral worth or an assertion that every individual agrees. Later drafts include a friendly octopus conductor and later game rosters include many categories. Do not convert a society's stereotype into a universal biological law.

R01 dramatizes anti-amphibian prejudice: Koric conceals himself in a cloak and is attacked when exposed. R03 has tribal captors seeking his blood. N02 treats an owl speaking with Veratus as rare and holy. These are related motifs, but their exact institutions and time periods remain draft-dependent.

## 7. Politics, money, work, and language

Older Sapporo versions use **job boards**, civilian citizenship, trade, and debates over permanent armies and taxation (T14, T16, A05). T14 uses **karma** as money; A01 quotes a whale fare in **sal**; G01 and Critz game documents use **bells**. These are source-specific currencies, not a confirmed multi-currency financial system or exchange rate.

T14 proposes increased citizenship fees and an annual tax to defend against Mogul's forces. This is character/political draft material, not an endorsed statement about real taxation or a decision that the same government exists in every age.

The strongest recurring story concerns are Veratus's identity and heritage, Koric's motives and responsibility, prejudice, friendship/found family, ambition, freedom, and redemption. Their recurrence is a reading of T05/T06/T10/T13 and the manuscripts, not proof that every proposed ending remains canon.
