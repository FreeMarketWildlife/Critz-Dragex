## Established story

**Koric**, a fire salamander, and **Veratus**, a basilisk lizard, journey together to return **Moana's Tear** to the center of **Death's Valley**. Its restoration brings rain and life back to the desolate land. Their successful return **ends the Third Age**, frees Koric from the condition preventing him from dying, and the formerly barren valley becomes the fertile **Kingdom of Moana**, ruled by **Veratus at the dawn of the Fourth Age**.

These are author-confirmed outcomes, not evidence that the complete final manuscript has been drafted. The archived sixteen-chapter 2023 draft ends before the Tear is restored.

## The author and recipient within the fiction

The text of *Moana's Tear* is written **by Archaíos during Age 4 as he nears death**. He is a first-generation Fey-blooded tortoise, among those who personally drank the blood-tainted river after the final Fey battle. He lives on **Eden's Isle**, west of Hokkaido. Each year migratory birds help send his letters to the mainland. Knowing he only has one more annual opportunity, he writes to **Olivia (“Olive”)**, his extraordinarily distant female descendant, aged **19**, who aspires to be a historian.

Olive attends the famous school and Great Library **inside the Grandfather Tree in the middle of Grimroot Grove** under a legendary owl. The exact number of generations between Archaíos and Olive is not known. Whether the book is framed as one letter, a collection, or an attached account is not yet established.

## Opening journey and Koric's deception — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct answers during the Moana's Tear author interview.

### Confirmed direction

- Koric is seeking **Veratus specifically**, knows his name before meeting him, and conceals that prior knowledge.
- Koric's actual business in **Sapporo** is delivering Veratus to the **Guild of Kalos**. What the guild wants with him and Koric's compensation remain unspecified in this interview.
- Koric draws Veratus into the journey by suggesting he knows where other basilisks are and offering to take him to **Mount Yokai** to reconnect with his ancestry. He presents himself as having business in Sapporo; the precise cover occupation is not settled.
- **Koric genuinely believes Veratus is the last living basilisk in the entire world. He therefore believes he is lying when he offers hope of meeting other basilisks.** Whether other basilisks actually survive is unknown; his belief does not establish their extinction as objective canon.
- The basilisk kingdom on Mount Yokai fell earlier in Age 3. Rootport's inhabitants are comparatively uninformed about this history, and many do not appreciate how rare basilisks have become.
- Veratus initially **refuses the call**. He ultimately accompanies Koric by boat; the specific event that changes his mind is still open.
- Later, at an **inn in Prairie Bridge**, with **Moto** present, their stated intention of meeting basilisks at Mount Yokai prompts one of Koric's friends to expose that Koric knows the basilisk kingdom has been erased. Exact dialogue and the friend's identity remain unwritten.
- Veratus loses trust and refuses to continue straight to Sapporo, insisting on **Mount Yokai** instead. Koric agrees to accompany him. The original immediate itinerary is Sapporo; when Koric initially promises the mountain visit relative to Sapporo is not settled.
- Phil describes **Moto as a small white field mouse**, with a samurai/ninja character concept. This supersedes the uncertain shrew-versus-mouse description for the current interview direction.

### Weston sequence and unresolved choices

- Phil's explicitly preferred chapter boundary is **Weston the whale swallowing the boat at the end of Chapter 1**, with Koric and Veratus surviving.
- Chapter 2 opens with a waterslide-like passage into a dark, wet, slimy chamber inside Weston.
- Boat size, passenger count, other passengers' fates, attacking sea creatures, and the precise rescue/admission sequence remain unsettled. Phil also explored shipwreck, capsizing, and entering the water before Weston collects them; these are alternatives, not additional consecutive events.
- Koric knows an octopus inside Weston, but the octopus's current name remains undecided: Phil tentatively recalled **Arnold**; recovered drafts use **Alfred**. Do not adopt Octavius or silently finalize a rename.
- Koric engineering a wreck to supply captives, treasure, or slaves was brainstorming only, **not confirmed**.
- Assistant suggestions about a small passenger vessel, a departure deadline, an affectionate farewell, and Veratus's specific feelings about his guardians remain proposals unless Phil confirms them.

## Ice Blade, guild sacrifice and Tear possession — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct author interview account, following the opening-journey decisions.

### Confirmed character knowledge and guild plan

- **Koric believes the Guild of Kalos will try to sacrifice Veratus to activate an ancient royal artifact**, provisionally called the **Ice Blade**. He undertakes the delivery despite that expectation, in pursuit of returning Moana's Tear.
- The Guild of Kalos **possesses Moana's Tear, or at least part of it**. This possession connects delivering Veratus with Koric's quest. The precise bargain, amount handed over, and whether payment is actually honored remain unspecified.
- This resolves the earlier interview's open questions about Koric's expected treatment of Veratus and the guild's connection to the Tear. A planned sacrifice is not confirmation that it succeeds.

### Royal blade and storm mechanism

- **Ice Blade is a working descriptor, not a finalized proper name.**
- The artifact is an ancient sword wielded by the **kings of Mount Yokai**. It has a hilt/handle that produces an **ice blade when activated**; it wields **ice and lightning** power.
- A life-taking killing blow with the sword, which sends the victim's spirit away, triggers **lightning from the sky**.
- **Thunderclouds form before the fatal blow**, in anticipation of the death. Phil connects this foreknowledge with omniscience/knowledge of past, present and future; the responsible being and exact mechanism are not settled.
- The blade's power contributed to the basilisks' conquest of Mount Yokai and their fearsome reputation.
- Basilisk rulers deliberately **kept prisoners to execute with the sword**, exploiting its storm effects. Approaching armies sometimes withdrew when thunderclouds formed around the mountain.
- The account describes wicked royal abuse of this power; it does not establish that every basilisk or every ruler was evil. Lightning's exact target, range, controllability and conditions beyond the stated fatal-blow trigger remain unspecified.

### Proposals and unresolved mechanism

- Phil suggests an ancient **yokai** may have given the sword to the basilisks and that a yokai may inhabit it. Fey or other magical-creature origins were also considered. **None of these origins or identities is finalized.**
- Phil considers involvement by **Allohim** or omniscient spiritual beings in the anticipatory storms; this is a possibility, not a confirmed divine endorsement or settled cosmology.
- A possible yokai subtype/name sounded like “tenoshi” in dictation; its spelling and meaning remain unconfirmed. Do not silently turn it into a named entity.
- Phil is considering **several parts/fragments of the portable Tear**, with **Veratus, Koric and Moto** each carrying some. Fragment count, dimensions, weight, division history and individual ownership are not settled.
- The large portable Tear concept is discussed in connection with Moana's immense skull. **Moana's Tear island is the skull landmass; it is not Eden's Isle** (Phil immediately corrected that verbal slip). The exact physical/magical relationship between the island and the portable Tear remains open; no carrying of the entire skull-island is established.
- Historical versions with different artifact acquisition routes remain preserved as drafts. Guild possession is now confirmed, but neither a completed exchange nor a particular heist outcome is thereby finalized.
