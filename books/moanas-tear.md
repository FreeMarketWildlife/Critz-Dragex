## Established story

**Koric**, a fire salamander, and **Veratus**, a basilisk lizard, journey together to return **Moana's Tear** to the center of **Death's Valley**. Its restoration brings rain and life back to the desolate land. Their successful return **ends the Third Age**, frees Koric from the condition preventing him from dying, and the formerly barren valley becomes the fertile **Kingdom of Moana**, ruled by **Veratus at the dawn of the Fourth Age**.

These are author-confirmed outcomes, not evidence that the complete final manuscript has been drafted. The archived sixteen-chapter 2023 draft ends before the Tear is restored.

## The author and recipient within the fiction

The text of *Moana's Tear* is written **by Archaíos during Age 4 as he nears death**. He is a first-generation Fey-blooded tortoise, among those who personally drank the blood-tainted river after the final Fey battle. He lives on **Eden's Isle**, west of Hokkaido. Each year migratory birds help send his letters to the mainland. Knowing he only has one more annual opportunity, he writes to **Olivia (“Olive”)**, his extraordinarily distant female descendant, aged **19**, who aspires to be a historian.

Olive attends the famous school and Great Library **inside the Grandfather Tree in the middle of Grimroot Grove** under a legendary owl. The exact number of generations between Archaíos and Olive is not known. Whether the book is framed as one letter, a collection, or an attached account is not yet established.

## Opening journey and Koric's deception — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct answers during the Moana's Tear author interview.

### Confirmed direction

- Koric is seeking **Veratus specifically**, knows his name before meeting him, and conceals that prior knowledge.
- Koric's actual business in **Sapporo** is delivering Veratus to the **Guild of Kalos**. What the guild wants with him and Koric's compensation remain unspecified in this interview.
- Koric draws Veratus into the journey by suggesting he knows where other basilisks are and offering to take him to **Mount Yokai** to reconnect with his ancestry. He presents himself as having business in Sapporo; the precise cover occupation is not settled.
- **Koric genuinely believes Veratus is the last living basilisk in the entire world. He therefore believes he is lying when he offers hope of meeting other basilisks.** Whether other basilisks actually survive is unknown; his belief does not establish their extinction as objective canon.
- The basilisk kingdom on Mount Yokai fell earlier in Age 3. Rootport's inhabitants are comparatively uninformed about this history, and many do not appreciate how rare basilisks have become.
- Veratus initially **refuses the call**. He ultimately accompanies Koric by boat; the specific event that changes his mind is still open.
- Later, at an **inn in Prairie Bridge**, with **Moto** present, their stated intention of meeting basilisks at Mount Yokai prompts one of Koric's friends to expose that Koric knows the basilisk kingdom has been erased. Exact dialogue and the friend's identity remain unwritten.
- Veratus loses trust and refuses to continue straight to Sapporo, insisting on **Mount Yokai** instead. Koric agrees to accompany him. The original immediate itinerary is Sapporo; when Koric initially promises the mountain visit relative to Sapporo is not settled.
- Phil describes **Moto as a small white field mouse**, with a samurai/ninja character concept. This supersedes the uncertain shrew-versus-mouse description for the current interview direction.

### Weston sequence and unresolved choices

- Phil's explicitly preferred chapter boundary is **Weston the whale swallowing the boat at the end of Chapter 1**, with Koric and Veratus surviving.
- Chapter 2 opens with a waterslide-like passage into a dark, wet, slimy chamber inside Weston.
- Boat size, passenger count, other passengers' fates, attacking sea creatures, and the precise rescue/admission sequence remain unsettled. Phil also explored shipwreck, capsizing, and entering the water before Weston collects them; these are alternatives, not additional consecutive events.
- Koric knows an octopus inside Weston, but the octopus's current name remains undecided: Phil tentatively recalled **Arnold**; recovered drafts use **Alfred**. Do not adopt Octavius or silently finalize a rename.
- Koric engineering a wreck to supply captives, treasure, or slaves was brainstorming only, **not confirmed**.
- Assistant suggestions about a small passenger vessel, a departure deadline, an affectionate farewell, and Veratus's specific feelings about his guardians remain proposals unless Phil confirms them.

## Ice Blade, guild sacrifice and Tear possession — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct author interview account, following the opening-journey decisions.

### Confirmed character knowledge and guild plan

- **Koric believes the Guild of Kalos will try to sacrifice Veratus to activate an ancient royal artifact**, provisionally called the **Ice Blade**. He undertakes the delivery despite that expectation, in pursuit of returning Moana's Tear.
- The Guild of Kalos **possesses Moana's Tear, or at least part of it**. This possession connects delivering Veratus with Koric's quest. The precise bargain, amount handed over, and whether payment is actually honored remain unspecified.
- This resolves the earlier interview's open questions about Koric's expected treatment of Veratus and the guild's connection to the Tear. A planned sacrifice is not confirmation that it succeeds.

### Royal blade and storm mechanism

- **Ice Blade is a working descriptor, not a finalized proper name.**
- The artifact is an ancient sword wielded by the **kings of Mount Yokai**. It has a hilt/handle that produces an **ice blade when activated**; it wields **ice and lightning** power.
- A life-taking killing blow with the sword, which sends the victim's spirit away, triggers **lightning from the sky**.
- **Thunderclouds form before the fatal blow**, in anticipation of the death. Phil connects this foreknowledge with omniscience/knowledge of past, present and future; the responsible being and exact mechanism are not settled.
- The blade's power contributed to the basilisks' conquest of Mount Yokai and their fearsome reputation.
- Basilisk rulers deliberately **kept prisoners to execute with the sword**, exploiting its storm effects. Approaching armies sometimes withdrew when thunderclouds formed around the mountain.
- The account describes wicked royal abuse of this power; it does not establish that every basilisk or every ruler was evil. Lightning's exact target, range, controllability and conditions beyond the stated fatal-blow trigger remain unspecified.

### Proposals and unresolved mechanism

- Phil suggests an ancient **yokai** may have given the sword to the basilisks and that a yokai may inhabit it. Fey or other magical-creature origins were also considered. **None of these origins or identities is finalized.**
- Phil considers involvement by **Allohim** or omniscient spiritual beings in the anticipatory storms; this is a possibility, not a confirmed divine endorsement or settled cosmology.
- A possible yokai subtype/name sounded like “tenoshi” in dictation; its spelling and meaning remain unconfirmed. Do not silently turn it into a named entity.
- Phil is considering **several parts/fragments of the portable Tear**, with **Veratus, Koric and Moto** each carrying some. Fragment count, dimensions, weight, division history and individual ownership are not settled.
- The large portable Tear concept is discussed in connection with Moana's immense skull. **Moana's Tear island is the skull landmass; it is not Eden's Isle** (Phil immediately corrected that verbal slip). The exact physical/magical relationship between the island and the portable Tear remains open; no carrying of the entire skull-island is established.
- Historical versions with different artifact acquisition routes remain preserved as drafts. Guild possession is now confirmed, but neither a completed exchange nor a particular heist outcome is thereby finalized.


## Phoenix curse and Cave of Origins — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct author interview account.

### Koric's soul, lives and dreams

- Koric's **phoenix curse** causes repeated lives: **the same soul enters a new body with a new mind**. He remains the **same species (fire salamander)** and **always male**; Phil considered then explicitly rejected varying his sex.
- **Every death damages and hurts the soul**, progressively worsening its condition. The curse perpetuates death and suffering rather than granting desirable immortality. Phoenixes also exist in this world; their exact relationship to Koric's curse is not established.
- Throughout his current life, Koric dreams of his previous lives reaching out to him. The dreams tell him the cycle of death and suffering will continue and convince him that **returning Moana's Tear is his destiny and overriding life's task**.
- This clarifies that the previously recorded inability to die means an inability to escape the rebirth cycle / achieve final death, not that his individual bodies cannot die.
- Do not invent the curse's original caster, original offense, number or length of lives, rebirth timing, or a universally applicable soul system for all creatures.

### Cave geography and memory experience

- **Cave of Origins** is near Mount Yokai, closer to **East Lake**, which lies roughly **southeast of Mount Yokai**. Phil describes **twin rivers running from Mount Yokai into East Lake and West Lake**. Exact routes and coordinates are unspecified; this does not automatically replace the separately mapped great rivers.
- Koric and Veratus visit the cave **either shortly before or after Mount Yokai**; the order is undecided.
- Koric undergoes an intense spiritual experience in which his mind receives the memories/information of **all his previous lives**. His memory becomes fully filled out.
- **After this experience, the dreams cease.** Phil described torn or separated soul aspects as a tentative analogy; do not treat this as a settled literal soul-fragment anatomy, complete soul healing, or lifting of the curse.
- Koric tells Veratus **not to follow him inside**. When he takes too long, Veratus enters after him.
- Koric tackles Veratus out of the cave, beats/attacks him, and they have a major fight extending into the **river**. The cause of Koric's aggression, degree of control, and precise choreography remain open.

### Legendary bird intervention — proposed

- Phil proposes that a legendary bird called **Drakos** intervenes, stops their fight, and tells them they must work together.
- The name was dictated as **D-R-A-K-O-S with an accent**, but the accent's letter and form were not specified. **Drakos is an unaccented working transcription**, not a settled accented spelling.
- **Species undecided:** raven favored over an eagle; identity and intervention remain proposed.
- Repository search for “Drakos” returned no matches during this interview. Recovered character notes separately contain **Enáretos** (raven), **Díkaios** (peregrine falcon), **Draco** (lizard), and **Drago** (antagonist). **No identity or rename relationship is established** by the similar names. Do not merge them automatically.

This author account governs over incompatible historical phoenix/memory scenes. It does not settle the entire chapter order or alter the established Age 3 / Age 4 transition.

## Veratus's father, Koric's execution and soul reunion — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's direct continuation of the author interview. Obvious dictation variants normalize to Koric, Veratus, Ice Blade and the established Creator spelling Allohim; no explicit Creator rename was requested.

### Previous life and the king

- In Koric's **immediately preceding life**, he was **counselor to the king of Mount Yokai, Veratus's father**. That incarnation's name remains undecided.
- Veratus's father was an **evil, hypocritical king**: he outlawed magic while demanding that Koric use it for royal purposes, including saving Veratus's mother during childbirth and torturing people to extract information. These are demands; their outcomes and Koric's compliance in each case are not specified.
- The king **executed Koric with the Ice Blade**, using the royal execution/storm mechanism already described.
- Koric in that previous life was **morally upright**, had learned to tame his spirit and reunite his damaged soul, and had come very close to obtaining Moana's Tear. The precise chronology of those accomplishments is not yet supplied.

### Destruction of the basilisk kingdom

- That execution was the **tipping point** for a united assault on Mount Yokai, **led specifically by mammals**; the **hosts of heaven** also came.
- The attackers devastated the basilisk kingdom, burned the entire mountain, and **the earth shook**.
- Phil describes no basilisks or eggs remaining **on Mount Yokai** after the destruction. This establishes the destruction of that mountain kingdom, **not proven worldwide basilisk extinction**. Veratus survives; how he escaped, was removed, or was absent is not yet established.
- This is the later **Age 3 fall of the basilisk kingdom**, distinct from the **Age 2 Fey battle** on Mount Yokai. Do not conflate the two events or invent the cause of the shaking.

### Magic and persecution

- **Anyone can practice magic**, regardless of species. **Not every amphibian practices it**.
- Amphibians are culturally associated with magic, including practices perceived as witchcraft/voodoo, and suffer persecution/discrimination on that basis.
- Phil places the emergence of persecution after the Fall / Battle of Mount Yokai, following some interval when magic was accepted. Exact elapsed time, geographic reach, and the chronology of individual bans remain unspecified; the basilisk king's ban is confirmed.

### Literal soul fragments and Cave of Origins

- **When Koric dies, a fragment of his soul separates and finds its way to the Cave of Origins.**
- Some incarnations never discover the cave; others do. At the cave, those fragments **reunite with him**, restoring access to past lives. This now confirms literal soul fragmentation/reunion and **supersedes the earlier interview note treating soul-fragment language only as an analogy**.
- Soul reunion can overwhelm his control. The reference to multiple spirits entering him describes his own past-life fragments; do not invent unrelated possessing souls.
- Phil establishes that **a piece of Moana's Tear is in the Cave of Origins**, giving Koric a concrete reason to visit it.
- Phil's **tentative explanation** is that the immediately previous incarnation placed the piece there, anticipating that the king would murder him for refusing orders and preparing for his next life. The placer and exact preparation remain proposed, not finalized.

### Why Koric attacks Veratus

- During the cave reunion, Koric experiences the immediately preceding incarnation's murder. **Veratus strongly resembles his father**.
- The current Koric's hatred and consuming focus on the Tear interact with that returning memory: he sees Veratus as the threatening killer and believes he will prevent the Tear's return.
- Overwhelmed and unable to control himself during this event, Koric **tries to kill Veratus**. This settles the cause of the previously recorded cave/river fight.
- The previous incarnation's moral uprightness does not make the current incarnation equally controlled or morally mature.

### Raven and final death

- A **raven intervenes and stops the fight**. This now confirms raven species and intervention over the earlier raven-versus-eagle possibility.
- The raven knows **Veratus is destined to rule Moana's Tear, the actual location**, and the restored kingdom. Do not equate that island with Eden's Isle.
- The raven also knows that **Koric will die in Death's Valley**, achieving his **final death**, after which his spirit can go to **Allohim, Creator of the universe**. This resolves the earlier unspecified eventual death location and its finality; exact timing relative to the return, manner and scene remain open.
- The raven's intended name remains the working transcription **Drakos**, with the author-requested accent still unspecified. No identity/rename link to Enáretos, Díkaios, Draco or Drago is established.


## Cave-to-mountain order and campfire confession — October 14, 2026

Source: Phil's latest direct author interview decisions. These supersede the earlier undecided cave-before/after-mountain sequence.

### Confirmed sequence

1. After the **Prairie Bridge inn revelation**, Koric agrees to accompany Veratus to **Mount Yokai**, departing from the direct northward route to Sapporo. This concession is a way of regaining favor with Veratus.
2. **Koric's dreams intensify as they approach Mount Yokai**. One morning a particularly strong dream directs him to the **Cave of Origins near East Lake**, south of Mount Yokai.
3. Koric insists on this further detour because he feels strongly called by destiny; he tells Veratus he can continue toward Mount Yokai. Veratus is confused and upset at yet another change of direction. Exact dialogue and how he decides to accompany Koric remain unwritten.
4. **Cave reunion and river fight occur before the Mount Yokai visit.** Immediately after the attack, Veratus still does **not know why Koric tried to kill him**.
5. The **raven stops the fight**, tells them they are destined to **work together to restore Moana's Tear**, and directs them toward **Mount Yokai for answers**.
6. The raven tells Veratus he will be **king over Lake Moana**, using the lake's name rather than Death's Valley. Veratus is deeply confused and at this point knows nothing of his royal status.
7. **That night, before entering Mount Yokai**, Koric explains his immediately previous life at a campfire, including **Veratus's father murdering him**. This settles the timing of Veratus learning why Koric attacked.
8. **After the cave and campfire**, they enter Mount Yokai. Phil wants Veratus to discover/establish that **he is king in the king's chamber**. The discovery mechanism is undecided. How the campfire account of his royal father differs from the chamber's revelation of Veratus's own status requires clarification; do not silently invent concealment or a magical proof.

### Campfire chapter and literary frame

- Phil wants **an entire chapter centered on the campfire telling of Koric's immediately previous life**.
- Koric also tells many stories of other lives that night. Phil proposes **Archaíos's narrative aside** explaining that his letter has no room to recount them all, while directing the recipient to **Veratus's own writings in a library at the restored lake/kingdom**.
- The exact aside, “thousands” as literal count versus rhetorical abundance, the library's name/location, and the format/extent of Veratus's writings remain tentative.
- This develops a **letter-oriented presentation**, but does not yet settle single-letter versus multiple-letter/attached-chronicle mechanics. Do not replace the established Grandfather Tree library with the proposed lake library or equate them without confirmation.

### Place names

- Phil explicitly **prefers Lake Moana** to “Moana's Lake” for the lake and the raven's language.
- The restored polity's final name remains open: **Kingdom of Moana** is the previously established working name; “Moanan Kingdom” and other variants are being considered, not finalized replacements.
- **Moana's Tear** remains the established name of the skull-island and portable artifact; those are not renamed by choosing Lake Moana.
